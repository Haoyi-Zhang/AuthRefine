# Literature comparison and reference counts

The comparison is purposive and organized around the actual obligations: bidirectional representation, authorization, refinement, certificate translation, dependence, and proof/implementation boundaries. It is not a census or a ranking of the journal. The 22 distinct full-paper comparisons are recorded in `literature_comparison.csv`: twelve same-journal methodological comparators, five separately selected high-visibility journal articles, and five adjacent-venue papers. Bibliographic metadata and the exact readable-copy locations are recorded in `external_resources.csv` and `literature_reference_counts.csv`.

## Direct competitors and the retained distinction

Margrave (Fisler et al., ICSE 2005) is a direct policy-difference comparator, not merely background on access control. It uses decision diagrams to analyze authorization and the semantic impact of changes. Dougherty, Fisler and Krishnamurthi (IJCAR 2006) are closer to the stateful question: contextual containment is restricted to accessible policy environments. Their argument already connects reachable local judgments with executions. The present result does not originate that principle. It assumes a common target graph whose observations reconstruct the entire source state for every admitted binding, then characterizes each binding's earliest discrepancy by minimum target distance.

DynPAL (Becker, CSF 2009) supports policy-changing actions, reachability and invariants in a richer update language. UCON (Park and Sandhu, 2004) treats mutable attributes and ongoing usage conditions. Zelkova (Backes et al., FMCAD 2018) and Block Public Access (Bouchet et al., ESEC/FSE 2020) show that semantic permission comparison and query-relative simplification are established authorization-analysis techniques. Cedar's published PACMPL 2024 article supplies a typed, side-effect-free language and symbolic analysis. Its publication replaces the earlier preprint citation; the work is counted once.

NetKAT, its coalgebraic decision procedure, symbolic language-equivalence algorithms, and bisimulation up to congruence are the relevant algorithmic alternatives to exhaustive products. KATch (PACMPL 2024) adds a modern symbolic implementation. Relational NetKAT (Xu et al., PACMPL 2026) uses a specified relation to describe network changes, including changes to packet traces that are not equal-length lockstep transformations. The present equality criterion does not subsume that trace-relation language. Conversely, those publications do not automatically establish the fixed-reference failure-profile theorem for this observation model. The comparison is semantic and methodological, not a claimed performance ranking across incompatible workloads.

Table `tab:closest` separates the compared object, relation and state information for the most direct approaches. Each row is linked to a specific source passage in `reference_audit.csv`. The other publications support distinct obligations: state refinement and auxiliary variables; proof-carrying code and translation validation; formal controlled syntax; abstraction, counterexample refinement and dependence; or the distinction between preservation, rights safety and enforcement. The journal-calibration sample remains documented even when a calibration paper is not needed in the manuscript bibliography.

## Reading depth and publication metadata

The citation audit distinguishes a main-text passage from an abstract-only reading and from an inherited source-reading record. It does not turn a publication record into a claim of having verified every theorem. Newly inspected full-text passages include the dynamic-policy, Margrave, DynPAL, access-calculus, Zelkova, KATch, complete-state/trace-set and Relational NetKAT comparisons. Some foundational comparisons use a publisher or author-written abstract for a correspondingly narrow statement; those rows say so explicitly. Existing source-verification records are retained rather than presented as newly repeated readings.

Publication metadata takes precedence over an incidental date or footer in an author copy. The Existence of Refinement Mappings is cited as the 1991 journal article, while its available author report is an earlier access copy. The access-control calculus is TOPLAS 15(4), despite an inconsistent author-copy footer. Guarded Kleene Algebra with Tests belongs to the PACMPL 2020 POPL issue although online availability preceded that issue. Cedar's published author list and article identifier are used. Preprints still cited as preprints, including CNL-P and AutoCedar, are not assigned an unverified peer-reviewed status. No inaccessible page count or DOI is invented.

## Scope of the result and executable evidence

Functional refinement, parsing/printing laws, certificate translation, symbolic family analysis and dependency slicing are established methods. The retained result is the conditional local certificate and binding-wise target-distance characterization for the specified state-revealing interface. The observable quotient, projected-state closure and restricted binding family delimit its transfers. Switching closure is a direct consequence of the same invariant: full-state uniformity implies agreement even when bindings change between requests. Fixed binding remains the meaning of an interpretation and its negative witness, not a necessary premise for that stronger positive property.

The interpretation-family corollary separates a candidate that implements every meaning, only a nonempty proper family, or no meaning. The last case does not establish source ambiguity. A correct compilation at a known binding cannot fall in that last case. These are mathematical consequences, not a new family-enumeration implementation or an automatic permission to change a specification.

The implemented symbolic alternative can eliminate support that syntactic viability retains. No universal speed advantage is claimed. Public evidence consists of six small model/policy pairs, deterministic projections and explicitly constructed stateful extensions. It supports adapter and checker behavior, not deployment or workload representativeness. The unchanged original Cartesian/full-state experiments and written proofs provide different kinds of evidence; neither is described as proof-assistant verification of the executable code.
